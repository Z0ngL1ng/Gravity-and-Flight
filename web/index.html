01 / A changing research question

From adding knowledge
to organizing capability.

Our earlier work focused on capability coverage: giving a model the remote-sensing knowledge and skills it needed before specialization. As the backbone grows stronger, our research shifts toward a new question: how can it organize what it already knows to solve more demanding tasks?

Recognizing ships, vehicles, and facilities is a starting point. A useful judgment must select the observations that matter, connect them to the task, and adapt when the local evidence changes. This is the step from FBA to Gravity & Flight.

↓

Knowing what is there is the beginning.
Knowing what matters is the next step.

The connection to FBA

From scene transfer
to context transfer.

Filling Before Advancing (FBA) prepares the capabilities needed for scenario specialization. Its second stage, Bridging-Conv, learns shared priors from target and related bridging scenes. This scene-transfer idea is the starting point for G&F.

We bring the transfer question inside a scene domain: when two images belong to the same broad category, how should judgments adapt to changes in local attributes, spatial relations, or visibility? G&F studies these fine-grained contexts under a shared task.

Reading the G&F method figure

Gravity checks visual truth against images and Evidence Cards. Flight evaluates descriptions and analyses using the public task and permitted comparison metadata; it receives no answer contract or reference judgment, and is not gated by Gravity.

02 / The evidence

Same question.
A different reading.

Two distinct harbor images, one shared question. Each image is answered independently; their local evidence determines what the judgments should preserve, change, or leave less specific.

Shared question

How are the vessels positioned relative to the waterfront?

=

Keep what both images support.

Vessels, waterfront structures, and shore-side buildings appear in both images. These broad observations remain supported; an identical operating function does not follow.

≠

Reconsider the spatial relation.

In A, the vessel sits inside a narrow enclosure. In B, vessels lie along a more open basin. The spatial description should change with the local evidence.

≈

Make the claim no stronger than the evidence.

These views alone do not establish active repair, facility throughput, or operational intensity. Describe the visible structure while leaving unsupported activity uncertain.

Illustrative reading of the repository images. These descriptions are not model outputs or benchmark results.

03 / The design inspiration

An idea borrowed from aerodynamics.

From gravity and lift to visual grounding and reasoning: a physical intuition becomes two complementary verification views.

The design draws inspiration from the interplay of gravity and aerodynamic lift. Gravity anchors visual claims to the image; Flight evaluates how reasoning develops from those observations and adapts across task-matched contexts.

The sketch expresses the naming metaphor. Its arrows do not encode reward weights.

Gravity

G1 / G2

“Does the image support what was said?”

Visual fidelity
Checks visual claims throughout the response against the image and its Evidence Card.
Observation coverage
Checks the initial description for salient subjects, attributes, and relations. Later analysis cannot fill a missing observation.

Flight

F1 / F2

“Does the reasoning follow from the description?”

Evidence use
Checks whether the analysis uses described facts under the task’s target, conditions, and scope.
Paired adaptation
Checks task execution on both sides and whether the evidence supports consistency, change, or a narrower claim.
↳

Flight accepts described premises conditionally. It does not inspect images or Evidence Cards. Gravity checks visual truth; a separate Task Check assesses final answers.

04 / The learning process

How G&F learns.

Read the framework from top to bottom: supervised initialization, then relational policy optimization.

Training details & objective＋

Four steps, from sampling to update

  1. 01

    Sample independently

    The same policy generates K = 6 responses per image from that image and the shared question.

  2. 02

    Match & verify

    Random one-to-one matching forms response pairs. Gravity, Flight, and Task Check evaluate their prescribed views.

  3. 03

    Share the advantage

    Combine the scores, normalize within the candidate group, and assign one pair-level advantage to both responses.

  4. 04

    Update one policy

    Both sides receive equal loss weight. KL regularization uses the frozen Stage-1 reference, outside the reward.

Rₖ = rₖtask + ½ ∑ᵢ vᵢ,ₖ [λGGᵢ,ₖ + λFFᵢ,ₖ]

The pair reward adds task correctness and weighted Gravity and Flight scores. The validity indicator v checks the description → analysis → answer structure. There is no Gravity gate on Flight in this formulation.

Âₖ = (Rₖ − μ) / (σ + ε)

The same group-normalized advantage updates both responses. Evidence Cards, Boundary Cards, and comparison annotations supervise verification; they are never included in the policy prompt.
